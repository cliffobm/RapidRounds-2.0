## rr-neuro-loc-01

Describe drowsiness using an observed response to voice.

Format: single. Difficulty: foundation.

An adult opens their eyes when the nurse says their name, answers orientation questions correctly, and follows commands. When conversation stops, the patient drifts back to sleep. Which note best communicates this assessment?

- **rr-neuro-loc-01-coma: Comatose; no further response testing is needed.**

  Eye opening, appropriate answers, and command following contradict this description.

- **rr-neuro-loc-01-drowsy — KEY: Drowsy, arouses to voice, answers correctly, and follows commands.**

  This records both the stimulus needed and the response observed.

- **rr-neuro-loc-01-normal: Alert throughout the assessment; neurologic status is normal.**

  The patient does not sustain wakefulness after conversation stops.

- **rr-neuro-loc-01-obtunded: Obtunded; unable to respond appropriately to spoken requests.**

  The described patient does respond appropriately to voice; this note changes the findings.

Takeaway: Pair an arousal descriptor with what the patient actually does.

Key cues: Opens eyes to name; Answers correctly; Drifts back to sleep

Mechanism: Wakefulness and the quality of a response are separate observations. A useful note preserves both.

Transfer reflection: How would your note change if the patient opened their eyes but gave unrelated answers?

## rr-neuro-loc-02

Distinguish a change in arousability from a change in terminology.

Format: single. Difficulty: application.

At 0800, a patient described as lethargic opens their eyes to a normal speaking voice and answers promptly. At 0830, repeated louder verbal prompts are needed, responses are delayed, and attention cannot be sustained. Breathing is currently effective. Which interpretation is most appropriate?

- **rr-neuro-loc-02-same: Both notes describe sleepiness, so the neurologic assessment is unchanged.**

  The strength of stimulation and quality of responses have changed.

- **rr-neuro-loc-02-coma: The later assessment establishes coma regardless of the remaining responses.**

  The patient still responds; this label overstates the observed finding.

- **rr-neuro-loc-02-worse — KEY: Arousal has decreased; document the response and obtain prompt clinical evaluation.**

  The trend is concerning even before a single descriptor is selected.

- **rr-neuro-loc-02-label: Select the preferred term before reporting any change to the clinical team.**

  Debating terminology should not postpone communication of the deterioration.

Takeaway: A new need for stronger stimulation is a meaningful trend.

Key cues: Previously responded to ordinary voice; Now needs repeated louder prompts; Delayed responses

Mechanism: Terms such as lethargic and obtunded vary between teaching resources. The observed response makes the change interpretable.

Transfer reflection: What baseline hearing or communication information would you verify while assessing this change?

## rr-neuro-loc-03

Calculate and report a fully testable adult GCS.

Format: single. Difficulty: foundation.

An adult opens their eyes to speech, converses but is confused about location, and obeys a two-step motor command. All three GCS components are testable. Which recording is correct?

- **rr-neuro-loc-03-13 — KEY: E3 V4 M6 = 13.**

  Eye opening to sound is 3, confused conversation is 4, and obeying commands is 6.

- **rr-neuro-loc-03-14: E4 V4 M6 = 14.**

  E4 requires spontaneous eye opening; speech was needed here.

- **rr-neuro-loc-03-12: E3 V3 M6 = 12.**

  The patient converses despite disorientation; isolated words would be a different response.

- **rr-neuro-loc-03-15: E4 V5 M6 = 15.**

  This would misrecord both eye opening and orientation.

Takeaway: Record E, V, and M alongside the total when all components are testable.

Key cues: Eyes open to speech; Confused conversation; Obeys commands

Mechanism: Each observed response has its own score. A total alone hides which component changed.

Transfer reflection: What would you report if an endotracheal tube prevented verbal assessment?

## rr-neuro-loc-04

Preserve a not-testable GCS component without fabricating a total.

Format: single. Difficulty: advanced.

An intubated adult opens their eyes spontaneously and obeys commands. The endotracheal tube prevents a verbal response. Which statement follows the structured GCS approach?

- **rr-neuro-loc-04-one: Record E4 V1 M6 and a total of 11 because no speech is heard.**

  An obstruction to testing is different from an observed absent response.

- **rr-neuro-loc-04-infer: Record E4 V5 M6 because command following establishes normal verbal ability.**

  Motor responses do not establish an untested verbal response.

- **rr-neuro-loc-04-omit: Omit the full neurologic assessment until the tube has been removed.**

  The eye and motor findings can still be assessed and trended.

- **rr-neuro-loc-04-nt — KEY: Record E4 V-NT M6, explain the tube limitation, and do not calculate a total.**

  NT preserves the testing limitation while keeping the usable components visible.

Takeaway: Do not replace a not-testable response with an invented number.

Key cues: Endotracheal tube; Eyes open spontaneously; Obeys commands

Mechanism: A misleading total can make a testing barrier look like a neurologic deficit.

Transfer reflection: Which other treatment or communication factors could limit interpretation of a component?

## rr-neuro-loc-05

Recognize an acute change from an established neurologic baseline.

Format: single. Difficulty: application.

A patient hospitalized after stroke had clear speech and equal, reactive pupils at 0900. At 0905, speech becomes slurred and the right pupil is larger and sluggish. Which nursing response is most appropriate?

- **rr-neuro-loc-05-wait: Repeat the routine assessment at the next scheduled interval before reporting.**

  The new focal findings require a response now.

- **rr-neuro-loc-05-activate — KEY: Activate the urgent stroke or rapid-response pathway and assess ABCs concurrently.**

  The acute change requires immediate evaluation while physiologic threats are assessed.

- **rr-neuro-loc-05-rest: Reduce conversation and let the patient rest before comparing the pupils again.**

  Rest does not explain or address these new findings.

- **rr-neuro-loc-05-history: Finish a comprehensive health history before contacting the stroke team.**

  A full history should not delay response to an observed acute change.

Takeaway: New focal findings require immediate escalation, even without a complete diagnosis.

Key cues: Clear speech five minutes earlier; New slurred speech; New pupil asymmetry

Mechanism: Changes in more than one neurologic finding strengthen concern for an evolving problem; they do not establish its cause.

Transfer reflection: Would the need for urgent evaluation change if the speech became clear again before the team arrived?

## rr-neuro-loc-06

Prioritize breathing when LOC decreases.

Format: single. Difficulty: application.

A patient with decreased LOC has snoring respirations at 8/min and SpO₂ of 88%. Another nurse is activating the emergency response. What should the bedside nurse prioritize?

  Scoring must not postpone response to ineffective breathing.

- **rr-neuro-loc-06-water: Give water to determine whether swallowing improves with stimulation.**

  Oral intake adds aspiration risk in this situation.

- **rr-neuro-loc-06-airway — KEY: Support airway and ventilation within training and protocol while help arrives.**

  The current airway and breathing findings require immediate support.

- **rr-neuro-loc-06-family: Obtain a detailed family history before taking further bedside action.**

  History is useful but does not address the immediate physiologic threat.

Key cues: Decreased LOC; Snoring, slow respirations; SpO₂ 88%

Mechanism: A detailed neurologic examination cannot replace support for inadequate breathing.

## rr-neuro-loc-07

Identify factors that can confound neurologic response testing.

Format: multiple. Difficulty: application.

Which factors should the nurse identify when interpreting a patient’s neurologic responses? Select all that apply.

- **rr-neuro-loc-07-hearing — KEY: Hearing aids are unavailable during spoken instructions.**

  A missed instruction may reflect hearing rather than arousal.

- **rr-neuro-loc-07-sedation — KEY: A sedating medication was recently administered.**

  Treatment timing can affect the observed response.

- **rr-neuro-loc-07-language — KEY: The examination is conducted in a language the patient does not understand.**

  A language barrier can interfere with a response to commands.

- **rr-neuro-loc-07-baseline — KEY: Baseline aphasia affects spoken answers.**

  A baseline communication deficit must be described when interpreting responses.

- **rr-neuro-loc-07-ignore: Any new change can be ignored if one confounding factor is present.**

  A confounder calls for interpretation and adaptation; it does not rule out deterioration.

Takeaway: Document barriers and adapt assessment while still evaluating new changes.

Key cues: Communication access; Recent medication exposure; Known baseline

Mechanism: A response depends on the ability to receive and carry out the request as well as arousal.

Transfer reflection: How could you adapt a spoken instruction without assuming a patient has impaired consciousness?

## rr-neuro-loc-08

Plan hydration safely when swallowing status is uncertain.

Format: multiple. Difficulty: foundation.

Following an acute stroke, a patient is drowsy and has not completed a swallowing screen. Which actions fit a safe plan to promote adequate hydration? Select all that apply.

- **rr-neuro-loc-08-npo — KEY: Keep oral food, fluids, and medications withheld until swallowing safety is established.**

  Water and tablets also require a safe swallowing route.

- **rr-neuro-loc-08-route — KEY: Clarify the prescribed hydration and medication routes with the team.**

  Hydration needs remain while oral intake is restricted.

- **rr-neuro-loc-08-water: Offer small sips of water because the patient can speak.**

  Speaking does not establish safe swallowing.

- **rr-neuro-loc-08-assess — KEY: Assess intake, output, and the patient’s hydration status.**

  The plan should reflect the individual assessment.

- **rr-neuro-loc-08-unlimited: Replace the restriction with unrestricted oral fluids when the patient reports thirst.**

  Thirst alone does not remove aspiration risk or determine fluid requirements.

Takeaway: Hydration language must specify a safe route and an individualized plan.

Mechanism: A goal to maintain hydration does not itself authorize oral intake or a particular fluid volume.

# Lesson rr-neuro-loc-lesson-01

Describe and respond to a change in LOC

Use precise arousal descriptions, component findings, and a time-stamped trend to guide your next nursing action.

Estimated duration: 10 minutes.

Objectives:

- Differentiate arousal descriptions using observed responses.

- Interpret GCS components alongside pupils, speech, and motor findings.

- Prioritize airway safety, prompt escalation, and clear documentation when LOC decreases.

## Teaching step 1

Start with the baseline: how the patient usually opens their eyes, communicates, and follows commands. Identify hearing, language, speech, medication, and other factors that could affect testing.

## Teaching step 2

Describe the stimulus and response. “Difficult to arouse” is more useful when followed by the actual eye, verbal, and motor findings. Do not use “obtunded” as a substitute for describing what happened.

## Teaching step 3

Record GCS components using the structured assessment method taught and approved in your setting. Add the total only when all three components are testable. Pupils, laterality, and speech findings remain separate parts of the neurologic assessment.

## Teaching step 4

Compare exact times and findings. A decrease in arousal or a new focal deficit requires prompt assessment and escalation. Do not finish a long examination before calling for help when the change is urgent.

## Teaching step 5

Respond to airway and breathing threats while the team evaluates the cause. Individualize positioning and hydration; decreased LOC or uncertain swallowing safety can make oral intake unsafe.

## Teaching step 6

Reassess after interventions and communicate what changed, what you did, the response, and what still needs evaluation. Improvement does not authorize stopping the ordered plan.

## Arousal descriptors: always add the observed response

- **Alert:** Awake and readily engages. Document orientation separately; an awake patient may still be confused.

- **Lethargic / drowsy:** Sleepy but can be aroused with voice; may drift back to sleep. Record whether responses are prompt and sustained.

- **Obtunded:** Reduced alertness with more stimulation needed and slower or limited responses. Specify the actual stimulus and response.

- **Stuporous:** Arousal is brief and requires vigorous stimulation. Assessment technique belongs within supervised training and local policy.

- **Comatose:** Cannot be aroused into wakefulness. Record observed motor and reflex responses separately; the term does not establish brain death.

Descriptor boundaries vary between texts. Use your course definitions consistently and document observable findings; do not assign a GCS from a descriptor alone.

## Adult GCS: eye opening

- **E4:** Eyes open without a stimulus.

- **E3:** Eyes open when addressed by sound.

- **E2:** Eyes open in response to appropriate pressure.

- **E1:** No eye opening, with testing possible.

Use NT and explain the reason when this component cannot be tested.

## Adult GCS: verbal response

- **V5:** Conversation shows correct orientation.

- **V4:** Conversation is possible, but the patient is confused.

- **V3:** Recognizable words without conversational exchange.

- **V2:** Vocal sounds without recognizable words.

- **V1:** No verbal response, with testing possible.

An endotracheal tube can make verbal testing unavailable. Do not infer V5 from command following.

## Adult GCS: best motor response

- **M6:** Carries out a requested movement.

- **M5:** Moves purposefully toward the site of a pressure stimulus.

- **M4:** Flexes normally away from pressure without localizing it.

- **M3:** Shows an abnormal flexion pattern.

- **M2:** Shows an extension pattern.

- **M1:** No motor response, with testing possible.

Document right/left differences separately. Use the official structured assessment aid for technique; this teaching chart is not a bedside competency assessment.

## Report components and context

- **Fully testable example:** E3 + V4 + M6 = 13. Include the observed criteria with the numbers.

- **A component is NT:** Report the available components and reason for NT; do not calculate a total.

- **The total is unchanged:** Compare components, pupils, speech, and motor symmetry. An unchanged total can hide a new deficit.

GCS describes responses. It does not independently diagnose a cause or replace clinical assessment.

## Worked example

At 0800, a patient opens their eyes spontaneously, is oriented, and follows commands. At 0830, their eyes open to voice and conversation is confused, although commands are followed. E4 V5 M6 = 15 has changed to E3 V4 M6 = 13. Describe and urgently communicate the change while assessing ABCs and other possible contributors; do not explain it away as sleepiness.

## Reflection

Why is “opens eyes to voice, gives incorrect month, follows commands” more useful than “neuro unchanged”? What finding would make you activate an immediate response?
